::: zh
**响度战争（loudness war）** 指唱片工业长期追求「比对手更响」的倾向：用压缩与限幅把动态压扁，让作品在电台、电视与播放列表里显得更突出。八十年代 CD 出现后，这场竞争愈演愈烈。

代价是瞬态变软、失真增加、听众疲劳；整张专辑听下来缺少起伏。流媒体按 LUFS 归一化后，做得更响会被整体压回，靠限幅换来的优势基本消失，但母带仍需在目标响度与动态之间取舍。

常见误解是响度战争已经结束。归一化削弱了动机，却没有消除「在同等响度下更饱满」的竞争；**通行说法**是保留动态比争峰值更耐听。
:::

::: en
The **loudness war** is the record industry's long push to be louder than the competition, using compression and limiting to flatten dynamics so a track stands out on radio, TV and playlists. The trend intensified after CDs arrived in the 1980s.

The cost is softer transients, more distortion and listener fatigue; an album loses its ups and downs. Streaming platforms normalize by LUFS, so a louder master is turned down as a whole and the advantage gained through limiting largely disappears. Masters still balance target loudness against dynamics.

The usual mistake is declaring the war over. Normalization weakens the motive but not the contest to sound fuller at equal loudness; the common view is that keeping dynamics ages better than chasing peaks.
:::
