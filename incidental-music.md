::: zh
**戏剧配乐（incidental music）** 是为话剧、广播剧等非歌剧舞台演出写的音乐，放在幕前、幕间、换景或台词之间的空隙里。它不承担唱段，而是用序曲、间奏曲、舞曲与气氛段落补足戏剧的时间。

十九世纪这类音乐常由一部戏催生出一整套管弦乐，如门德尔松为《仲夏夜之梦》所作；其中若干段后来编成组曲单独演出。二十世纪电影配乐吸收了它的写法，舞台演出则保留现场演奏的传统。

听戏剧配乐，要把它放回戏剧结构：哪一段在换景，哪一段在等观众入场，哪一段替台词留白。**它的成败不在旋律是否动听，而在是否让舞台的时间顺滑地过去。**
:::

::: en
**Incidental music** is written for spoken theatre, radio drama and other staged productions, filling the gaps before the curtain, between acts, during scene changes or under dialogue pauses. It has no sung roles; overtures, entr'actes, dances and atmospheric numbers measure the play's time.

In the nineteenth century a single play often produced a whole orchestral score, as with Mendelssohn's music for A Midsummer Night's Dream; selected numbers were later arranged as a concert suite. Film scoring absorbed its methods in the twentieth century, while the stage kept live performance.

To hear incidental music, put it back into the play's structure: which passage covers a scene change, which waits for the audience to sit, which leaves space after a line. **Its success lies not in a memorable tune but in making stage time pass smoothly.**
:::
