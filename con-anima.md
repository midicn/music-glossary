::: zh
**有灵魂地（con anima）** 字面「带着灵魂」：anima 源自拉丁语，兼有气息与灵魂两义。它要求音乐**有内在的生命**，句子往前长，而不是靠加速制造热闹。

它容易和 animato 混淆：后者是「活跃起来」，重点在动作；con anima 的重点在**呼吸与热力**，速度不变，音乐却显得活。**常见误解是把它弹成 con brio**——光辉与冲劲是另一回事。

在浪漫派作品中它常与行板并用，提醒演奏者别把歌唱性处理得四平八稳。可与 [[glossary:animato]]、[[glossary:con-brio]] 对读。
:::

::: en
**Con anima** literally means "with soul": anima comes from Latin, where it means both breath and soul. The music must have **inner life**, phrases growing forward rather than excitement manufactured by extra speed.

It is easily confused with animato. Animato means "animated", and the emphasis falls on motion; con anima puts the weight on **breath and heat**, so the tempo may stay put while the music comes alive. **The usual mistake is to read it as con brio** — brilliance and drive are another matter; soul is lit from within.

Romantic scores often pair it with andante or a moderate pace, a warning not to let the singing line settle into routine. Compare animato and con brio, which look after motion and brilliance while anima looks after the soul.
:::
