::: zh
**协和（consonance）** 与**不协和（dissonance）** 描述一个音程或和弦听起来是「稳」还是「需要解决」。八度、纯五度、大三度通常被听成协和；小二度、三全音、大七度通常被听成不协和。声学上，这与两个音的频率比、以及泛音是否「对得上」有关。

但**边界随时代移动**：中世纪把三度当成不协和，文艺复兴以后它成了最常用的协和音程；爵士把七和弦当作家常便饭，古典时期它却是需要准备与解决的不协和。**协和不是一条物理定律，而是一套听觉习惯。**

::: en
**Consonance** and **dissonance** describe whether an interval or chord sounds "stable" or "needs to resolve". Octaves, perfect fifths and major thirds are usually heard as consonant; minor seconds, tritones and major sevenths as dissonant. Acoustically this relates to the frequency ratio of the two notes and whether their overtones line up.

But **the border moves with history**: the Middle Ages treated the third as dissonant, the Renaissance made it the most common consonance; jazz treats seventh chords as ordinary, while the Classical style treated them as dissonances to be prepared and resolved. **Consonance is not a law of physics but a habit of hearing.**
