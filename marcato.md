::: zh
**强调地（marcato）** 来自意大利语 marcare「标记」，通行说法追溯到日耳曼语 markōn（划界、做记号）。它要求把音**一个个标出来**：起音明确，音与音之间有清楚的界。

它描述**轮廓的清晰度**，不是速度，也不等于更响。谱上可以写 piano marcato：轻声，但每音分明。**常见误解是把 marcato 当成「加音量、砸下去」**——真正的 marcato 靠起音的时点与音头的形状；它与重音记号也不同，重音标一次，marcato 是一种持续的奏法。

::: en
**Marcato** comes from the Italian marcare, "to mark"; the usual account traces it to Germanic markōn, "to mark a boundary". It asks that notes be **marked out one by one**: a clear onset and a clear edge between them.

It describes **clarity of outline**, not speed, and not louder sound. A page can say piano marcato — quiet, yet every note distinct. **The usual mistake is to treat marcato as "more volume, strike down"** — real marcato lives in the timing of the onset and the shape of the attack; it also differs from an accent sign, which marks one note, while marcato is a continuing manner.
