::: zh
**音色（timbre）** 是让同一个音高听上去是两件不同乐器的东西。钢琴与小提琴同奏 A4，频率一样、响度也可以调得一样，耳朵仍能立刻分开——区别在**分音的强弱分布**（基音之上叠着哪些泛音、各自多响）、**起音与衰减的形状**（击弦、拉弓、吹气留下不同的音头），以及**噪声成分**（弓毛摩擦、气息湍流）。

所以音色不是一张固定标签：同一把提琴换一根弦、换一个把位，音色就变了。

常见误解是把音色等同于「音质」——前者是乐器本身的声音身份，后者是录音与重放系统的保真度，两件事。

::: en
**Timbre** is what makes the same pitch sound like two different instruments. Play A4 on a piano and a violin: the frequency is identical and the loudness can be matched, yet the ear separates them at once. The difference lies in **the balance of partials** (which overtones sit above the fundamental and how loud each is), **the shape of the attack and decay** (a hammer, a bow and a breath each leave a different onset), and **the noise content** (bow hair scraping, turbulent air).

So timbre is not a fixed label: change a string or shift to another position on the same violin and the colour changes.

The usual mistake is to confuse timbre with audio fidelity — the first is an instrument's sonic identity, the second is how faithfully a recording chain reproduces it. They are different things.
