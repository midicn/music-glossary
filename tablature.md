::: zh
**指法谱（tablature）** 不直接写音高，而是写手怎么放：几条横线通常代表琴弦，线上的字母或数字代表品位；管乐器也有类似的指孔记法。文艺复兴的鲁特琴谱用字母，现代吉他六线谱用数字，思路相同。

它省去了「这个音在哪根弦上」的换算，照谱就能按弦；代价是同一音高在不同把位有不同写法，换调弦或换乐器便失效，早期指法谱还常不标节奏。

今天吉他谱常把六线谱与五线谱并排：五线谱给音高与节奏，六线谱给位置。把 TAB 当成「简化的五线谱」是常见误解，它记录的是动作，不是音级关系。
:::

::: en
**Tablature** does not write pitches directly; it writes what the hand does. Lines usually stand for strings, and the letters or numbers on them give frets; wind instruments have comparable hole diagrams. Renaissance lute books used letters, modern guitar TAB uses numbers, but the idea is the same.

It removes the work of deciding which string carries a pitch: read the sign and place the finger. The cost is that one pitch has several positions, so the notation depends on tuning and instrument, and early sources often leave rhythm unmarked.

Guitar music today often prints TAB beneath the staff: the staff carries pitch and rhythm, TAB carries position. Calling TAB a simplified staff is a common mistake; it records actions, not scale-degree relationships.
:::
