::: zh
**朴素地（semplice）** 来自拉丁语 simplex，字面「一重、不折叠」，与复杂相对。它要求**不加修饰地把话说完**：音色干净，分句自然，不靠夸张的弹性取巧。

朴素不是空洞，也不是随便。**常见误解是把 semplice 弹得没有主张**——真正的朴素需要极强的控制：每个音的位置都清楚，句子有起有落，只是藏起痕迹。

它常与 cantabile 连用，写成 semplice e cantabile，提醒演奏者先歌唱、再谈风格。可与 [[glossary:dolce]]、[[glossary:cantabile]] 对读。
:::

::: en
**Semplice** comes from Latin simplex, literally "onefold, not folded", the opposite of complex. It asks the player to **say the thing plainly, without decoration**: clean colour, natural phrasing, none of the effects borrowed from exaggerated rubato or accent.

Plain is neither empty nor careless. **The usual mistake is to play semplice without opinions** — true plainness takes strong control: every note in place, phrases with rise and fall, and the effort hidden.

It often pairs with cantabile as "semplice e cantabile", a reminder to sing first and style second; it is especially common in slow movements of the Classical period. Compare dolce and cantabile.
:::
