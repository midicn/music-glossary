::: zh
**主题（theme）**是一段完整的音乐陈述：它有清楚的开始、走向和收束，能独立成立，也能被记住、被认出。与动机相比，主题更像一句话而不是一个词；它通常包含一个或几个动机，并把它们组织成有方向的整体。奏鸣曲、赋格、变奏曲的开头，都要先把主题说清楚，后面的发展才有依据。

主题不等于「主旋律」。在复调作品里，主题会依次出现在几个声部；在变奏曲里，主题会先以和声骨架或低音线的形式存在；在大型作品里，主题甚至可以由几个性格不同的乐思组成。**判断主题的关键，是它是否构成一个可辨认的完整陈述**，而不是它是否由某一件乐器从头奏到尾。主题被拆开、转调、压缩、倒影之后，留下的往往是动机层面的东西。

常见误解是把「主题」和「动机」当成同义词，或者反过来以为主题必须很长。实际上二者是层级关系：动机是最小的可变形单位，主题是包含动机的完整陈述；一个主题可以只有四小节，也可以长到几十小节。另一个误解是以为主题一旦出现就必须原样重复；主题的生命恰恰在于它能否在变奏、发展和再现中保持身份。**若一段材料改完之后再也认不出来，它更接近动机素材，而不是主题本身。**
:::

::: en
**A theme** is a complete musical statement: it has a clear beginning, a direction and a close, and it can stand on its own, be remembered and be recognised. Compared with a motive, a theme is more like a sentence than a word; it usually contains one or more motives and organises them into a shape with momentum. Sonata, fugue and variation movements all state their theme first, because everything that follows needs that statement as a point of reference.

A theme is not the same as "the main tune". In polyphonic music the theme passes from voice to voice; in a set of variations it first exists as a harmonic frame or a bass line; in a large work it can be built from several ideas of different character. **What makes it a theme is that it forms a recognisable complete statement**, not that one instrument plays it from beginning to end. Once a theme is split, transposed, compressed or inverted, what remains is often material at the level of the motive.

The common misconception is to use "theme" and "motive" as synonyms, or to assume that a theme must be long. In fact the two are levels of one hierarchy: a motive is the smallest unit that can be transformed, while a theme is a complete statement built from motives; a theme may last four bars or several dozen. Another misconception is that a theme must return unchanged. Its life lies in whether it keeps its identity through variation, development and recapitulation. **If material becomes unrecognisable after rewriting, it was motive material rather than a theme.**
